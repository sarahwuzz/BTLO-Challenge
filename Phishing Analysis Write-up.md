# Phishing Email Analysis – Blue Team Labs Online Write-up

## Executive Summary
This write-up covers the analysis of a phishing email challenge provided by Blue Team Labs Online (BTLO). The investigation was conducted using **PhishTool** to analyze the email header metadata, rendering, and source structure.

## Investigation Details

### Question 1: Who is the primary recipient of this email?
**Answer:** `Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk`

### Methodology & Findings
During the initial investigation, an interesting discrepancy was identified across different sections of PhishTool:

1. **Rendered Tab:** Displays `Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk` in the visible `To:` header of the rendered email body.
2. **Details Pane (PhishTool Left Sidebar):** Lists the recipient as `johnsmith123@gmail.com`.
3. **Source Tab (Raw Email Headers):** The `Delivered-To` header explicitly indicates `johnsmith123@gmail.com`.

### Analysis & Explanation

#### Understanding the Email Structure

To understand why `johnsmith123@gmail.com` appears in the `Delivered-To` header while `Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk` appears in the rendered body, we must analyze the nature of the email artifact.

#### 1. The Email Type: Non-Delivery Report (Bounce Email)
The sample provided is a **Bounce Email** (also known as a *Non-Delivery Report* or *NDR*), automatically generated and sent by a **Mailer-Daemon** (Mail Transfer Agent). 

#### 2. Analyzing the Recipient Discrepancy
* **Original Intended Recipient `Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk`:** 
  This was the original destination address where a previous email was supposed to be delivered. The rendered email body shows this address because it includes the attached/quoted content of the failed original delivery attempt.
  
* **Actual Mailbox / Investigator Perspective `johnsmith123@gmail.com`:** 
  Because the original email failed to reach Kinnar, the mail server's `Mailer-Daemon` generated a bounce notification and sent it back to the original sender—**John Smith**. 

#### Conclusion
In this scenario, from our investigative perspective, **John Smith** is the recipient of the system notification `Delivered-To: johnsmith123@gmail.com`, while **Kinnar** was the **primary target/intended recipient** of the underlying message that failed to deliver.

## Attack chain analysis

To understand what actually happened in this case, let's look at the underlying attack chain:

### 1. Initial access
- The adversary successfully compromised the blog hosting service website named `markgardner.com`
- Abusing contact form, leveraging the compromised site, the adversary utilized it's online contact form.

What evidence support this findings?

The evidence supporting this finding is the `X-PHP-Script` & `X-PHP Filename`header.
Those header are automatically added by the mail server, and confirms that the email was generated by PHP file script located within the `markgardner` website directory (index.php).

Additionally, inspecting the email attachment in the rendered tab clearly reveals field such as:
```
First Name: Robertbiolo
Last Name: Robertbiolo
Email Address: kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk
Mobile: 85471397431
Booking Date: 1977-11-10
Booking Time: RobertbioloXE
Services Required:

Good earnings from $6500 per day >>>>>>>>>> https://35000usdperwwekpodf.blogspot.sg?p=9swghttps://35000usdperwwekpodf.blogspot.co.il?o=0hnd

```

The contact form lacked basic anti-bot security controls (such as CAPTCHA or rate limiting). This vulnerability allowed the adversary to automate form submissions via a bot/script.

Within the rendered message fields and attachments, the adversary injected spoofed sender metadata (aliased as *Robertbiolo*) along with a malicious phishing link intended for the target victim (`Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk`).

### 2. Attacker Identification (Source Attribution)
* **Originating IP Address:** The raw headers explicitly capture the adversary's true client IP address in the `REMOTE_ADDR` parameter.
* **Analytic Reasoning:** Because the PHP script processes the web request upon form submission, `REMOTE_ADDR` reflects the direct IP of the client/bot that accessed the `markgardner` website and executed the HTTP POST request at that exact timestamp.

### 3. Execution & Delivery Failure (The Bounce Event)
* **Non-Delivery Report (NDR):** The outbound phishing attempt failed because the targeted recipient account (`Kinnar1975@yahoo.co.uk`) was inactive/defunct.
* **Redirection to Owner:** Consequently, the Mail Transfer Agent (`Mailer-Daemon`) generated an automated bounce notification. This notification was delivered to `johnsmith123@gmail.com` (likely the website administrator or domain owner), who ultimately received the alert containing the reflected payload.
